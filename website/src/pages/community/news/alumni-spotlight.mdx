*Jane Smith graduated in 2022 with a B.S. in Computer Science. She now works as a software engineer at a mid-sized fintech company in Pittsburgh. We caught up with her over video call to talk about the transition from Allegheny to industry.*

---

**What did your first year after graduation look like?**

Honestly, a little chaotic — in a good way. I had two offers and spent a lot of time talking to my advisor before choosing the one I did. The first six months were a steep learning curve. The codebase was enormous, the tooling was different from anything I'd used in class, and the pace was fast. But I felt prepared in the ways that actually mattered: I knew how to read unfamiliar code, I knew how to ask good questions, and I wasn't afraid to say I didn't know something.

**What from Allegheny stuck with you?**

The writing. I know that sounds strange, but the emphasis on documentation and clear communication in every CMPSC course — lab reports, code comments, design docs — that carried over directly. I write better technical proposals than most of my colleagues who came from larger programs, and I think that's because at Allegheny, writing was never separate from the technical work.

The other thing was project-based learning. I wasn't just implementing toy problems. I was building things that had to actually work, that other people had to read and understand. That's what real software engineering is.

**Any advice for current students?**

Do the capstone seriously. I know it feels like a lot of overhead, but the experience of managing a project from idea to deployment — with real stakeholders, real constraints, and a real deadline — is something you can't manufacture in a homework assignment. Also: learn to use git like you mean it. Branching strategy, meaningful commit messages, code review etiquette. That's the first thing I noticed new engineers struggle with on the job.
