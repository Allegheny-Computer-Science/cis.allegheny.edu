Every spring, the CIS department recognizes students whose independent research projects have demonstrated exceptional creativity, rigor, and impact. This year's recipients represent the full breadth of the department — from theoretical computer science to human-centered design.

## This Year's Recipients

**Jordan Alvarez** earned recognition for their senior thesis on privacy-preserving machine learning, developing a federated learning framework that achieved near-identical accuracy to centralized models while keeping training data fully on-device. Their work has been submitted to an undergraduate research symposium.

**Priya Nambiar** received the award for a computational linguistics project exploring bias in large language model outputs across demographic groups. Priya's analysis surfaced patterns that have since been incorporated into a faculty research agenda on AI fairness.

**Marcus Chen** was honored for building an accessible web tool that translates CMPSC course data into visual, interactive prerequisite maps. The tool is already in use by incoming students during advising season.

## About the Award

The Student Research Award is presented annually to recognize undergraduate work that goes beyond coursework to make a genuine contribution — to the department, to a field, or to the broader community. Recipients are nominated by faculty and selected by a departmental committee.

Nominations for next year open in February. Students interested in independent research should speak with their advisor or any faculty member whose work interests them.
