# Computer & Information Science at Allegheny College

## Our Shared Values

Four values guide how we teach, mentor, and work together.

<img src="/img/values-cord.png" alt="Our Shared Values: Technical, Inclusive, Responsible, Ethical" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;margin:1.5rem 0;" />

- **Technical.** We aim to achieve competence and excellence in technical knowledge, its applications, and effects.
- **Inclusive.** We provide an inclusive community environment which invites and celebrates diversity of experience, thought, and belief.
- **Responsible.** We honor commitments and take responsibility for our actions and outcomes.
- **Ethical.** We make decisions rooted in principles of equity and justice, focusing on the greater good of the communities we impact.

Our [department policies](/about/policies) put these values into practice.

## Think Beyond the Code

**Computing means solving problems, creating possibilities, and understanding how technology affects people.**

Since 1979, Allegheny’s CIS department has connected computing with the liberal arts. Its program helped shape the Liberal Arts Computer Science “Model Curriculum,” reflecting a commitment to technical depth, clear communication, and thoughtful collaboration.

## Build Knowledge Through Practice

Our programs in **Computer Science, Data Science, Informatics, and Software Engineering** connect theory with hands-on experience. Students build a foundation in programming, data structures, algorithms, computer architecture, and software systems.

Nearly every course includes a formal laboratory component. Students put their knowledge to work by building applications, analyzing data, developing open-source tools, and working with robots and mobile devices. Team projects and interdisciplinary work strengthen their ability to solve problems together.

## Learn in a Connected Community

Small classes and close faculty relationships support individual attention, mentorship, and collaboration. Dedicated laboratories in Alden Hall provide space for coursework, software development, and advanced research.

These experiences extend beyond campus through internships with organizations such as **IBM, PNC, and MITRE**, as well as faculty-guided research that can lead to publications and presentations at international conferences.

## Consider the Human Impact

As students learn to build technology, they also examine its social, ethical, and professional consequences. The liberal arts broaden their perspective, helping them consider whose needs a system serves and how its design affects others.

**Can we build it? Should we? Who might it affect?** These questions connect technical decisions to our shared values.

## Shape What Comes Next

Together, technical foundations, practical experience, and a liberal arts perspective prepare students for careers and advanced study.

Our goal is to develop thoughtful engineers, creative problem-solvers, rigorous researchers, effective collaborators, and responsible leaders ready to **understand technology, build it, and help shape its future**.
